Does Trackability-Based Motion Curation Help a Small Biped?
Preliminary Report

ROB6323: Reinforcement Learning and Optimal Control for Autonomous Systems
Dhawal Kabra, Kanad Patel, Neer Patel, Pierrick Rugery
Repository: github.com/dk5426/rob6323-duck-motion-curation

1. Project description

A common way to give a legged robot many skills is to train one RL policy to track a library of reference motion clips. Real libraries contain bad clips: motions the robot cannot physically perform, and recordings with noise, glitches or sliding feet. ExBody2 proposed trackability-based curation: train a policy on every clip, measure how well it tracks each one, and drop the worst clips before training the final policy. The tracking error of a clip, however, mixes two things: how hard its motion type is, and whether the clip is broken. A clean but difficult walking clip can score worse than a broken bow, so plain curation may throw away exactly the hard, useful motions.

We propose type-normalised curation: each clip's score is divided by the typical score of clean clips of its own motion type, so a clip is judged only against its peers. Our goal is to show, in a controlled and pre-registered study on the Open Duck Mini v2 biped in simulation, that type-normalised curation improves tracking over training on the full library, over random removal and over plain curation, and to test whether this holds across RL algorithms.

2. Tools

  • Simulator: MuJoCo with its JAX version MJX, through MuJoCo Playground, for thousands of parallel environments on one GPU.
  • Robot and existing code: Open_Duck_Playground (Open Duck Mini v2 model, a 14-joint, roughly 40 cm tall open-source biped; its motion-tracking environment; and a Placo-based walk generator for reference gaits). We extend the environment with a per-clip sampler, a reference feed-forward action and evaluation tools.
  • RL libraries: Brax PPO and SAC; FastTD3; our own ARS and DAgger implementations.
  • Motion data: our own clip generator (inverse kinematics and keyframing on the robot model) plus physics checks (Section 5).

3. Algorithms

  • Tracking policy. The policy observes proprioception (joint angles and velocities, IMU, previous action) and the reference for the next frames. Its action is a residual on the reference: the joint target is qref(t+1) + 0.25 a, rate-limited to the servos' speed. The reward sums imitation terms (joints, keypoints, base pose and velocity) and regularisers. Training uses PPO (200M steps).
  • Tracking error per clip, averaged over frames and rollouts (3 per frame after a fall):
    e = joint error / 0.10 rad + keypoint error / 0.02 m
    (scoring adds base position / 0.05 m + heading / 0.2 rad + tilt / 0.1 rad)
  • Selectors that keep a fraction f of the training clips: full (keep all); random; kinematic (keep clips passing a joint-limit and speed check); curated (ExBody2: three scoring policies trained on all clips, keep the lowest ensemble error); and type-normalised (ours): s(c) = e(c) / median{ e(v) : v a clean validation clip of the same type as c }, keep the lowest s.
  • Other learners (Phase 2): PPO without the feed-forward, SAC, FastTD3, ARS and DAgger, to see whether the benefit depends on the algorithm. Alternatives to hard filtering (Phase 3): soft weighting of clips by score, failure-prioritised sampling, a difficulty curriculum, adaptive tracking tolerance, and repairing broken clips.

4. Planned work, experiments and expected results

  • Dose check. Train on the full library and on its clean clips only; the broken clips must raise validation error by at least 0.03, otherwise they are made more severe before the study.
  • Phase 1 (main experiment). PPO, 3 seeds per condition, keep-fractions 55, 65, 75 and 85% (main: 55%). Evaluation on held-out clean test clips (unseen parameter values, never used for any choice), 10 rollouts each, under nominal conditions and under pushes (0.3 and 0.6 m/s) and reduced friction. Statistics: differences in mean test error with 97.5% bootstrap confidence intervals over motion sources (Bonferroni over two hypotheses), plus a per-type completion check. Hypotheses: H1 type-normalised curation beats full and random; H2 plain curation beats full and random.
  • Phase 2. The best fraction for type-normalised curation (chosen on validation) with the other learners at equal wall-clock budget. Phase 3. The alternatives above against full and type-normalised curation.
  • Expected results. Plain curation removes most broken clips but also the clean clips of hard types (walking, turning, side steps), so tracking of those types degrades and H2 fails. Type-normalised curation keeps clean clips of every type while still removing broken ones, so H1 holds: lower test error and fewer falls than full and random. Impossible clips (balance, joint-limit, speed, take-off) should be easier to detect than corrupted ones (noise, glitches, foot skate), and off-policy learners (SAC, FastTD3), which replay bad data, should gain more from curation than PPO. All conditions, metrics, statistics and predictions are fixed in a pre-registration before training.

5. Preliminary work: the motion dataset

We built a 221-clip reference motion dataset for Open Duck Mini v2 (50 Hz, at most 4 s per clip), published with a GIF of every clip in the repository. It covers 14 motion types: forward and backward walking, side steps, turning in place, walking while turning (Placo gaits), leg lifts (march, stomp, kick, tap), crouch, bow, sway, head motions, hop, dance, headbang and twist (keyframed with inverse kinematics). Every clean clip passes kinematic checks (joint limits, servo speed, feet on the ground when in contact) and is no more demanding than the generator's own walking gaits on servo lag and balance margin.

The broken clips mimic real data problems and their labels are known by construction (used only for analysis, never by a selector). Impossible (each confirmed by a physics check in simulation): balance shove (the body is moved off its support; a static hold test fails), joint-limit violation, hops needing more take-off speed than the legs can give, time-compressed clips too fast for the servos, and one foot held up too long to balance. Corrupted: joint noise, short pose glitches, and a stance foot sliding while marked in contact.
